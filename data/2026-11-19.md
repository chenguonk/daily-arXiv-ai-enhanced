Abstract: We report the discovery of a massive outer companion around TOI-178, a known planetary system with six inner small planets within 0.15 AU, five of which are in a 2:4:6:9:12 resonant chain. We analyse the astrometric anomalies in the combined Gaia DR2/DR3 - Hipparcos catalogue (G23H) and literature radial velocities, revealing strong evidence for a massive outer companion at several AU. We combine the resulting orbital fit posterior with archival high-contrast imaging to constrain the properties of the corresponding massive outer companion, TOI-178 h. We also study the dynamical stability of the inner resonant chain in the presence of this previously unknown outer companion via both heuristic and N-body simulations. We find that the inner system appears to be robust to the perturbing influence of TOI-178\,h, in line with the systems several Gyr age. Our analysis results in a final posterior solution for TOI-178\,h with a semi-major axis of $6.9^{+12.3}_{-3.3}$ AU, a mass of $21^{+47}_{-11} M_J$, and eccentricity of $0.76^{+0.18}_{-0.35}$, likely corresponding to an eccentric brown dwarf. TOI-178 is currently the only known system that has maintained a high-multiplicity resonant chain whilst in the dynamical presence of a massive outer companion, showcasing a unique outcome of planet formation.

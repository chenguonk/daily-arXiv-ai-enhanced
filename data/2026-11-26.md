### [1] [The fate of Mercury's ejected mantle](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33297)
*Tuhin Ghosh,Mark Wyatt,Alan P. Jackson,Oliver Shorttle,Maximilian Sommer*

Main category: astro-ph.EP

TL;DR: 通过N体模拟结合碰撞演化模型，证明巨撞击抛射物再吸积效率低，从而支持水星大核的巨撞击起源。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 水星高体积密度指示异常高的核质量分数，巨撞击剥离幔层是合理解释，但若抛射碎片未能高效移除，它们会留在穿越水星轨道上并被再吸积，这构成该假说的关键困难。

Method: 将巨撞击抛射物的N体模拟与碰撞演化模型结合，并考虑作用于小尘埃的辐射压、电磁力等过程；区分"大块未汽化碎片主导"与"汽化后再凝结为毫米—厘米液滴"两种抛射物情形。

Result: 大碎片主导时碎片盘光学薄，碰撞级联在辐射压吹出极限处被截断，微米颗粒被移除；汽化情形下形成光学厚盘，级联可延伸至纳米颗粒，碰撞转为非碎裂耗散性，非弹性碰撞阻尼偏心率与倾角形成几何薄盘，表层纳米尘因高荷质比在太阳紫外下被电磁力快速清除。

Conclusion: 两种情形下碰撞演化都显著降低碎片再吸积，缓解了水星大核巨撞击起源的主要难题；更广泛地，行星系统内侧的高能巨撞击可能产生短寿命的纳米尘埃群。

Abstract: Mercury's unusually high core mass fraction, inferred from its high bulk density, remains difficult to explain. Mantle stripping by a giant impact provides a plausible origin, but without efficient removal, the ejected debris remains on Mercury-crossing orbits and later gets re-accreted. Here, we show that re-accretion of impact debris onto Mercury is inefficient, making a giant-impact origin plausible. We combine N-body simulations of giant-impact ejecta with a collisional evolution model and consideration of processes acting on small dust. We find that, when the ejecta are dominated by large, several hundred km-sized non-vaporized fragments, the debris disk remains optically thin and the collisional cascade is truncated near the radiation-pressure blowout limit, where micron-sized grains are removed. In contrast, if vaporized, the ejecta recondense into mm to cm-sized droplets and form an optically thick disk, in which radiation pressure cannot efficiently truncate the cascade. The debris can then be ground down to nanometre-sized grains, where collisions become predominantly non-fragmenting and dissipative rather than destructive. Subsequent inelastic collisions damp the eccentricities and inclinations of the nanodust, producing a geometrically thin disk whose lifetime is governed by removal through exposed surface layers, where grains acquire large charge-to-mass ratios through solar UV radiation and are rapidly removed by electromagnetic forces. In either scenario, collisional evolution strongly reduces the re-accretion of debris and alleviates a key difficulty for the giant-impact origin of Mercury's large core. More broadly, highly energetic giant impacts in the inner regions of planetary systems may lead to short-lived populations of nanometre-sized dust.
